# Human decisions and authority

## Three substantive gates

| Gate | Human decides | Required basis | If unresolved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1 — Problem | Target actor/task, desired outcome, scope, non-goals | Problem statement and explicit unknowns; permission to investigate is not proof of demand | Continue source organization; do not assert a settled requirement |
| G2 — Validation | Critical hypothesis, comparison, decision criteria, budget, allowed actions | Concrete experiment plan, evidence limits, environment/data prerequisites | Improve the plan; do not execute outside existing authorization |
| G3 — Decision | Adopt, revise, investigate, stop, or defer; permitted next scope | Observations tied to the plan, competing explanations, unresolved gaps | Preserve a recommendation as unconfirmed; do not begin implementation |

Record decision, scope, owner, date, rationale, and conditions. A prior clear authorization remains valid for its scope; do not ask again for each routine read or formatting change. A material change in scope, cost, data exposure, or external effect needs a new decision when not already authorized.

## Learning is separate from execution

"I would choose the cloud model" in a case is a learning answer. It does not authorize paid calls, transmission of data, configuration changes, deployment, or sending messages. In replay mode, gates concern the learner's proposed plan; label them `SIMULATED`. They do not create operational permissions.

Real experimental evidence cannot be approved into existence. An owner may accept a constraint, but absent observations remain absent. Unknown does not become PASS after confirmation.
